## Getting the Best Experience

<Accordion items={[
	{
		question: "What device should I use to watch?",
		answer: "<p>This one actually matters a lot. The Plex app on a <strong>Roku, Apple TV, Fire TV Stick, or NVIDIA Shield</strong> will give you the best experience by far. These devices can play most formats natively, which means the server doesn't have to work as hard.</p><p><strong>Smart TV apps</strong> (the Plex app built into your Samsung, LG, or Vizio TV) tend to be much worse. They often can't handle certain audio or video formats, which forces the server to convert the stream on the fly — a process called transcoding. This uses a lot of server resources, can cause buffering, and usually results in lower quality.</p><p>If you're watching on a smart TV and having issues, plugging in a $30 Roku or Fire Stick could be a game-changer.</p>"
	},
	{
		question: "What's transcoding and why should I care?",
		answer: "<p>Transcoding is when the server has to convert a video or audio stream in real time because your device can't play the original format. It's like the server running a translator between the content and your screen.</p><p>The problem is that it's resource-intensive. When multiple people need transcoding at the same time, the server can struggle. Direct play (where your device handles the file as-is) is always better for everyone.</p><p>The easiest way to avoid transcoding: use a good streaming device and set your quality to <strong>Maximum</strong> or <strong>Original</strong> when you're on Wi-Fi.</p>"
	},
	{
		question: "Why does the quality sometimes look bad?",
		answer: "<p>A few common culprits:</p><ul><li><strong>Your quality setting is too low</strong> — Check Plex settings and make sure video quality is set to Maximum or Original when on your home Wi-Fi</li><li><strong>Your device is forcing transcoding</strong> — Some devices (especially smart TV apps) default to lower quality. A dedicated streaming device fixes this</li><li><strong>Bandwidth issues</strong> — If your internet connection or Wi-Fi is spotty, Plex will automatically lower quality to avoid buffering</li><li><strong>The server is under load</strong> — If lots of people are streaming at once, quality might dip. This is rare but possible</li></ul>"
	},
	{
		question: "Can I download content for offline viewing?",
		answer: "<p>You can, but it requires a <strong>Plex Pass</strong> on your own account (a paid upgrade from Plex, not something Jory controls). With Plex Pass, you can download movies and shows to your phone or tablet for offline watching — great for flights or road trips.</p><p>Without Plex Pass, you'll need an internet connection to stream.</p>"
	},
	{
		question: "Why do some things have subtitles and others don't?",
		answer: "<p>Most content comes with subtitles included, but not everything. If you need subtitles for something and they're not there, let Jory know and he can usually add them.</p><p>Set your subtitle preference to <strong>Auto</strong> in the Plex app so they'll show up automatically for foreign language audio without you having to toggle them on every time.</p>"
